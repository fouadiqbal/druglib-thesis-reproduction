Abstract



The thesis addresses the creation of a machine learning- based drug classification model
according to their side effects. As there is a huge number of existing drugs and side effects
of each of them, an automatic system is highly demanded to assist healthcare professionals
and researchers in classifying and identifying drugs efficiently. In this paper, we discuss
the application of machine learning algorithms, namely Random Forest (RF), Logistic
Regression (LR), Support Vector Machines (SVM), and to classify drug side effects as
”Mild,” ”Severe,” or ”None.” The research uses a data set consisting of drug names, their
side effects, and additional features. Preprocessing operations on the data, including
imputation of missing values and encoding categorical variables, are performed to make
the dataset model-ready. Following the implementation and training of the models, their
performance is compared based on the standard classification evaluation metrics, such
as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. The findings confirm the effectiveness of the
application of machine learning algorithms for the classification of drugs into side effects,
with Random Forest performing best. This research illustrates the potential of machine
learning to enhance the task of drug classification, now being made more efficient and
user-friendly for application in pharmaceutical and medical practice. Future work will
be aimed at optimizing the models and integrating additional features for enhancing the
accuracy and stability of classification.

Introduction



1.1      Background


Drug classification is crucial for healthcare professionals, researchers, and patients to
understand the effects of medications. Accurate classification of drugs based on their
side effects helps in better prescribing practices, ensuring patient safety, and optimizing
treatment regimens.

Side effects are unintended reactions caused by drugs, which can range from mild to severe.
Managing side effects is critical in drug development, as they can significantly impact
patient adherence to prescribed treatments. The traditional method of categorizing drug
side effects often relies on manual labor, making it time-consuming and prone to human
error.

There are over thousands of drugs in the market, making it challenging for healthcare
professionals to memorize or track all potential side effects. The large and diverse volume
of drug data often leads to misclassification or missed adverse reactions. The lack of
efficient, automated systems for drug classification increases the workload for healthcare
providers and complicates decision-making.

Machine learning (ML) offers promising solutions for automating complex tasks like
drug classification by learning from data patterns. ML techniques, such as classification
algorithms, can categorize side effects into distinct groups, enhancing speed and accuracy.



                                             1
Models trained on large datasets can provide predictive capabilities, offering better insights
for drug side effect management and personalized healthcare.

Early prediction and classification of side effects can help mitigate the risks associated
with drug prescriptions, especially for vulnerable populations such as the elderly and those
with pre-existing conditions. An efficient drug classification system based on side effects
could lead to improved pharmacovigilance, which monitors, assesses, and prevents adverse
drug reactions.

With the increasing availability of healthcare data, machine learning can help uncover
trends and relationships that would be difficult or impossible to identify manually. By
leveraging ML, healthcare providers can make better-informed decisions, leading to safer
and more effective drug prescriptions.

This thesis proposes an automated drug side effect classification system using machine
learning algorithms. The goal is to develop a reliable and scalable model that can assist
healthcare professionals by classifying drugs and their side effects, ultimately improving
clinical decision-making and patient care.

1.2    Problem Statement


The rising volume of pharmaceutical drugs and the diverse side effects they cause present
significant challenges for healthcare. Currently, drug side effects are manually identified
and classified, leading to inefficiencies, errors, and delays in accessing critical information,
potentially affecting patient safety and treatment effectiveness.

By creating a machine learning model for the automatic classification of medications
based on adverse effects, this study addresses these issues. By grouping medications into
categories such as ”Mild,” ”Severe,” or ”None,” the system will improve classification
accuracy, reduce the amount of time needed for human processing, and help doctors
make prompt, well-informed prescription decisions. Its development was primarily focused
at addressing the weaknesses of present approaches, namely their inability to properly
identify dangerous side effects, in order to improve patient safety and healthcare delivery.

Chapter 2


Theoretical Overview



2.1      Drug Side Effects


2.1.1     Definition of Drug Side Effects:


Drug side effects refer to unintended or secondary effects caused by the administration of
a medication, which may vary in severity from mild to life-threatening. These side effects
can result from the pharmacological properties of the drug, patient-specific factors (e.g.,
genetic predisposition, age, etc.), and interactions with other medications.



2.1.2     Categories of Side Effects:


   • Mild Side Effects: These are typically manageable and may include symptoms like
        nausea, dizziness, or headaches.

   • Severe Side Effects: These can cause significant health issues and require medical
        intervention, such as allergic reactions, organ damage, or life-threatening conditions
        like anaphylaxis.

   • No Side Effects: Some drugs, in specific populations or dosages, might not cause
        any noticeable side effects.




                                               9
2.1.3      Importance of Identifying and Classifying Side Effects:


Proper classification of side effects allows healthcare professionals to make informed
decisions about which drugs to prescribe based on the patient’s medical history and
potential risks. It also aids in monitoring drug safety post-market, improving patient
outcomes by preventing adverse reactions.



2.2      Machine Learning in Drug Classification


2.2.1      Introduction to Machine Learning


Machine learning (ML) is a subset of artificial intelligence that uses algorithms to learn
from data and make predictions or classifications without explicit programming.In drug
side effect classification, ML algorithms can automatically process large datasets, identify
patterns, and predict side effects based on features like drug properties, patient information,
and historical data.



2.2.2      Types of Machine Learning:


Supervised Learning:


Involves training a model on labeled data where the output (e.g., side effect category) is
known. Algorithms learn to map input data (features) to these labels. Common algorithms
include:


   • Random Forest (RF): An ensemble learning method that builds multiple decision
        trees and combines their predictions for higher accuracy.

   • Logistic Regression (LR): A regression model used for binary and multi-class
        classification that estimates the probability of a particular class.

   • Support Vector Machine (SVM): A classification algorithm that works by finding a
        hyperplane that best separates different classes in the feature space.

                                               10
Unsupervised Learning:


Used when the data is not labeled, aiming to identify underlying patterns or groupings in
the data. This could be useful for clustering drugs with similar side effect profiles.


   • Reinforcement Learning: Not typically used in classification but may find applications
        in optimizing drug recommendation systems based on patient feedback.



2.2.3     Feature Engineering for Drug Classification


Features for classifying drugs may include both structured data (e.g., drug name, rating,
dosage) and unstructured data (e.g., textual side effect reviews). Text features are
processed using Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques, such as:


  1. TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency): A statistical method used
        to transform text data into numerical features, reflecting the importance of words
        in relation to the document and dataset.

  2. Word Embeddings: Techniques such as Word2Vec or GloVe, which represent words
        as dense vectors in high-dimensional space, capturing semantic meaning.



2.2.4     Model Evaluation Metrics


The effectiveness of the ML models is evaluated using various metrics:


   • Accuracy: The percentage of correct predictions over the total predictions.

   • Precision and Recall: Precision measures the accuracy of positive predictions, while
        recall assesses the ability of the model to identify all relevant instances of a class.

   • F1-Score: The harmonic mean of precision and recall, used to balance the trade-off
        between these two metrics.

   • Confusion Matrix: A table showing the performance of the classification model,
        particularly useful for visualizing how well the model distinguishes between classes.

                                               11
2.3     Challenges in Drug Side Effect Classification


2.3.1   Imbalanced Datasets


In real-world drug datasets, some side effect categories (e.g., ”Severe”) may have fewer
instances than others (e.g., ”Mild”), causing class imbalance. This can bias the model
towards the majority class. Techniques like oversampling, undersampling, and SMOTE
(Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique) are often used to balance the dataset and
improve model performance.



2.3.2   Data Quality and Missing Information


Incomplete or noisy data, such as missing side effect labels or inaccurate patient information,
can affect the accuracy of the model. Imputation methods or data cleaning techniques,
such as filling missing values with the most frequent category or using predictive models,
help address these issues.



2.3.3   Complexity of Drug Interactions


Drugs may interact in ways that influence side effects, making it challenging to capture
these interactions accurately in the model. Feature selection and interaction terms can
help identify relevant factors that influence side effects and improve model accuracy.



2.4     Applications of Machine Learning in Drug Side Effect Classification


Pharmacovigilance: ML models can aid in monitoring drug safety by detecting
patterns in side effect reports, helping regulatory bodies like the FDA and EMA make
better-informed decisions on drug approval or market withdrawal.

Personalized Medicine: By analyzing patient data (e.g., genetics, lifestyle), ML models
can predict which drugs are likely to cause side effects for specific individuals, leading to


                                              12
more tailored and safer treatments.

Real-Time Monitoring: ML-powered systems can analyze real-time patient data from

Literature Review



3.1    Overview of Drug Side Effect Classification


Drug side effects have been a critical focus in healthcare research for decades due to
the profound impact they can have on patients’ health and quality of life. Traditional
approaches to drug side effect classification rely heavily on expert knowledge, pharmacovigilance
systems, and clinical trials. However, these methods are often time-consuming and cannot
keep up with the vast volume of new drugs introduced to the market. Automated systems
using machine learning (ML) have emerged as a promising solution to this problem.



3.2    Traditional Methods for Side Effect Classification


Early studies in drug side effect classification primarily focused on manual methods, where
side effects were categorized by healthcare professionals based on clinical data and historical
reports. The World Health Organization’s Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) database and
FDA’s MedWatch system, for example, collect reports from healthcare providers and
patients. These databases, however, suffer from limitations such as incomplete reporting
and biased data [1]. Additionally, spontaneous reporting systems are often criticized for
underreporting serious ADRs, making it difficult to get a clear picture of drug safety.




                                              14
3.3    Machine Learning Approaches to Drug Classification


In recent years, machine learning (ML) has gained significant traction as a tool for
classifying drugs and predicting side effects. Supervised learning models, including
Support Vector Machines (SVM), Random Forest (RF), and Logistic Regression (LR),
have been applied to predict adverse reactions based on historical data. Li et al. (2018)
used an SVM-based approach to predict drug side effects and reported improved accuracy
compared to traditional methods. They used a variety of drug features, including chemical
structure and side effect reports, to train their model [2].

One significant advantage of ML-based methods is their ability to handle large datasets,
including text-based data, such as patient reviews and side effect reports, which are
difficult for traditional models to process. Yang et al. (2017) used a deep learning model
to classify drug side effects from clinical data and found that neural networks outperformed
conventional methods in terms of accuracy and predictive power [3].



3.4    Text Mining and Natural Language Processing (NLP) in
       Drug Side Effect Classification


A critical aspect of drug side effect classification is the analysis of textual data from
patient reviews and clinical reports. Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques,
such as TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency) and Word2Vec, have
been widely used to extract meaningful features from these unstructured texts. Zhang et
al. (2018) utilized TF-IDF to process patient reviews and classify drugs based on side
effects, showing that NLP techniques can significantly improve classification accuracy
when combined with traditional ML models [4].
Another breakthrough in this area is the use of deep learning for text analysis. Convolutional
Neural Networks (CNNs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) have been applied to
process and classify large volumes of unstructured medical text, providing deeper insights
into drug safety. Vaswani et al. (2017) introduced the Transformer model, which has since
been applied to a variety of NLP tasks, including drug classification [5]. Transformer-based


                                             15
models, such as BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers), have
shown state-of-the-art performance in understanding contextual relationships between
drug side effects and their related symptoms [5].



3.5    Challenges in Drug Side Effect Classification Using Machine
       Learning


While machine learning offers numerous advantages, several challenges remain in the
classification of drug side effects. One of the main challenges is dealing with imbalanced
datasets. In most drug datasets, severe side effects are much rarer than mild or no side
effects, which can cause classification models to be biased towards the majority class. He
et al. (2008) addressed this issue by using SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling
Technique) to balance the dataset, thus improving the performance of machine learning
models in detecting rare side effects [6].
Another challenge is the quality of data used for training. Drug side effect data, especially
when collected from spontaneous reporting systems or online platforms, can be noisy
and incomplete. Wang et al. (2017) demonstrated how data preprocessing techniques,
such as data cleaning and imputation, can significantly improve model accuracy in drug
classification tasks [7]. Ensuring that the data used for training is accurate and consistent
remains a crucial factor for the success of any ML model.



3.6    Applications of Drug Classification Models


Machine learning-based drug classification models have the potential to transform several
areas of healthcare. Pharmacovigilance is one of the primary fields where these models
can have an immediate impact. Automated systems can help regulatory agencies like
the FDA and the European Medicines Agency (EMA) track and analyze adverse drug
reactions more efficiently than traditional methods.
Furthermore, drug side effect classification models are being integrated into personalized
medicine. By analyzing a patient’s medical history, genetic data, and drug side effect
reports, ML models can predict which drugs are most likely to cause side effects for a

                                             16
specific patient. Bashir et al. (2020) used ML models to identify high-risk drugs for
specific populations, improving drug prescription safety and contributing to personalized
healthcare [8].



3.7    Gaps in Current Research and Contribution of This Thesis


Despite the progress in machine learning for drug classification, several gaps remain. Many
of the existing studies focus on narrow datasets or limited side effect categories, which
may not be representative of the entire range of potential side effects. Additionally, most
research has focused on supervised learning, with limited exploration of unsupervised
learning or semi-supervised learning techniques, which could be valuable for dealing with
incomplete or unlabeled data [13].
This thesis aims to address these gaps by developing a comprehensive drug side effect
classification system using multiple machine learning algorithms. The system will use
both structured (numerical data) and unstructured (textual data) features to enhance
the model’s performance. By exploring multiple ML techniques and evaluating their
performance on a large, diverse dataset, this research seeks to improve drug safety
monitoring and decision-making in clinical settings [14, 15].
